# Institutional and professional automated trading: who makes money, where, and why (as of November 2026)

Note on sourcing: most figures were taken from search-result summaries of the cited pages rather than full-text reads. Private-firm revenue figures (Jane Street, Citadel Securities, HRT, D.E. Shaw) come from press reports citing anonymous sources, not audited filings; Virtu (SEC 10-K) and XTX (UK company accounts) are the only filed figures here.

## 1. Which firms are the clearest examples of profitable automated trading, and what are their documented returns/revenues?

### Takeaway
Automated trading demonstrably makes very large sums at the professional level: a handful of market makers/HFT firms earned roughly $2bn to $40bn each in net trading revenue in 2025, and the best quant fund on record (Medallion) averaged about 66% a year gross for three decades. These are market-making/arbitrage businesses and closed, capacity-limited funds, not strategies sold to the public.

### Cited Findings
**Market makers / HFT firms (2025-2026)**
- Jane Street: record $39.6bn net trading revenue in 2025 (Q4 alone $15.5bn), per Reuters citing people familiar, 24 Apr 2026; firm declined to comment; gains on private stakes (incl. Anthropic) also boosted profit — [Reuters via KFGO](https://kfgo.com/2026/04/24/jane-streets-40-billion-trading-haul-tops-rivals-sources-say/); [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/jane-street-outpaces-rivals-with-record-40bn-trading-haul/)
- Jane Street reportedly booked over $40bn net trading revenue through July 2026, despite a July 2026 loss described as its first losing month since 2016 (secondary outlet; treat as unverified) — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/jane-street-surpasses-2025-revenue-despite-july-loss-1814942)
- Citadel Securities: about $12.2bn trading revenue in 2025, up 25% on 2024; Hudson River Trading: about $12.3bn in 2025 (same Reuters report, anonymous sources) — [Reuters via KFGO](https://kfgo.com/2026/04/24/jane-streets-40-billion-trading-haul-tops-rivals-sources-say/)
- Citadel Securities: record $7.3bn trading revenue in Q2 2026, net income $3.3bn (up more than 250%), per Bloomberg citing an unnamed person — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/citadel-securities-posts-record-7-3bn-trading-revenue/)
- Virtu Financial (public, audited): 2025 Adjusted Net Trading Income $2,145.3m, up 34.3% from $1,597.7m in 2024; average $8.6m per trading day (vs $6.4m in 2024); net income $912.3m (vs $534.5m); market making = $1,666.3m (78%) of ANTI — [Virtu FY2025 Form 10-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1592386/000159238626000009/virt-20251231.htm); [Q4 2025 earnings release, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1592386/000159238626000003/exhibit991q425.htm)
- Virtu's results rise with volatility and volume: the 34% jump in 2025 was attributed to market volatility — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/virtu-financials-adjusted-net-trading-income-jumps-34-as-market-volatility-spurs-activity/)
- XTX Markets (UK accounts): 2025 combined net profit across three UK entities about £1.71bn (2024: £1.28bn); revenue about £3.9bn, up 43-44%; roughly 250 staff; about $250bn daily volume. Outlets differ on the revenue definition (one gives net revenue £3.02bn vs £2.04bn) — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/brokers/xtx-markets-revenue-rises-43-to-393-billion-in-2025/); [FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/institutional/xtx-markets-sees-revenue-rise-44-in-banner-2025-topping-3-9-billion/)

**Quant hedge funds**
- Renaissance Medallion: 66.1% average annual return before fees and 39.1% after fees, 1988-2018, per the appendix of Zuckerman's "The Man Who Solved the Market" (source cited there: "Medallion annual reports; investors") — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2019/12/09/was-renaissances-success-luck-or-skill-and-was-it-behind-trumps-victory)
- Bradford Cornell's analysis of the same data: 66.1% is an arithmetic mean; compound return 63.3%; standard deviation 31.7%; $100 in 1988 would have grown to $398.7m by 2018 — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1k2fymby99nj0/Famed-Medallion-Fund-Stretches-Explanation-to-the-Limit-Professor-Claims)
- Medallion has been closed to outside capital since 1993 (employees/insiders only) — [Wealth Management (Bloomberg reprint)](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/alternative-investments/jim-simons-built-the-best-hedge-fund-ever)
- D.E. Shaw Composite fund: about +18.5% net in 2025; annualised net 12.9% since 2001 inception. Oculus: about +28.2% in 2025; 14.4% annualised net since 2004 (Reuters, anonymous source) — [Reuters via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2600609750)
- D.E. Shaw returns capital to investors (i.e. caps fund size) after strong years — [Bloomberg via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2500415947)

### Inferences
- The scale gap between firm types is itself informative: speed/scale-based market makers (Jane Street, Citadel Securities, HRT) earn tens of billions; one of the best institutional quant hedge funds open to outside money (D.E. Shaw Composite) compounds at about 13% a year net. A retail target of 5-10% per month (80-214% a year compounded) exceeds Medallion's net 39% a year.
- Capacity limits are visible: Medallion is closed, D.E. Shaw hands money back. The best automated edges do not scale and are not offered to the public.
- Market-maker revenue is not "prediction" profit; it is spread capture, arbitrage and inventory management across enormous volume, and it rises in volatile years (2025, 2026).

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 figures found for Two Sigma, Jump Trading or Tower Research; these firms are private and did not surface in searches.
- A search summary reported Hudson River Trading at $11.4bn trading revenue for Q2 2026 (more than 4x a year earlier), but I could not tie it to a specific article URL; treat as unverified.
- Medallion returns after 2018 not retrieved; the 1988-2018 series is unaudited (book appendix).
- Private-firm revenue figures are "net trading revenue" before compensation and other costs, not profit.

## 2. What share of trading volume is algorithmic/automated in US equities, futures, FX and crypto?

### Takeaway
Automation dominates every major electronic market: HFT alone is commonly put at around half of US equity volume, regulators say algorithmic traders are "well over a majority", and CFTC material puts futures automation above 70%. Precise current-year percentages from primary sources are scarce and definitions vary.

### Cited Findings
- US equities: a 2016 Congressional Research Service report cites estimates that HFT accounts for roughly 55% of US equity trading volume — [CRS R44443](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R44443.html)
- The SEC's "Staff Report on Algorithmic Trading in U.S. Capital Markets" (5 Aug 2020) concludes algorithmic trading has generally improved market quality and liquidity provision in normal conditions, though some types may worsen stress periods — [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/algo_trading_report_2020.pdf)
- Institutional (buy-side) flow: about 37% of 2023 US equity volume from institutions was executed via algorithms/smart order routers, up from 35% (Coalition Greenwich) — [The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/e-trading-platforms-experienced-increased-share-of-us-equity-trading-volume-in-2023/)
- Futures: a CFTC commissioner stated in a Nov 2016 open meeting that more than 70% of futures trading is automated (spoken remark) — [CFTC transcript](https://www.cftc.gov/idc/groups/public/%40newsroom/documents/file/transcript110416.pdf)
- Futures: CFTC staff found the share of automated orders rose in all commodity groups from 2013 to 2018, with financial products more automated than physical commodities — [CFTC press release 7901-19](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7901-19); product-level detail in [Haynes & Roberts, Update #2](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/2019-04/ATS_2yr_Update_Final_2018_ada.pdf)
- FX: electronic share of spot dealer-to-client trading was 75% (BIS, 2019) — [BIS Quarterly Review, Dec 2019](https://bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1912g.htm)
- FX: BIS Markets Committee published a dedicated report on FX execution algorithms (Oct 2020); a summary notes several large corporates use them for about 20-25% of their FX volume — [BIS mktc13](https://www.bis.org/publ/mktc13.pdf); [FXAlgoNews](https://fxalgonews.com/bis-reveals-prevalence-of-fx-execution-algorithms/)
- Latency races (pure speed competition) make up about 20% of FTSE 100 trading volume in exchange message data — [BIS Working Paper 955](https://www.bis.org/publ/work955.pdf)

### Inferences
- An individual's order in equities, futures or FX is almost always executed against, or alongside, professional algorithms; the counterparty is rarely another amateur.
- "Algorithmic" covers three different things that should not be conflated: execution algos (slicing a large order), HFT/market making (profit from spread and speed), and systematic alpha strategies (profit from prediction).

### Gaps
- No reliable, primary-source figure found for the bot/algorithmic share of crypto volume. Vendor claims exist but none were verifiable.
- The frequently quoted "TABB Group: HFT about 50% of US equity volume since 2010, peak about 61% in 2009" and "70% of EBS spot FX orders are algorithmic (BIS)" appeared only in search summaries; I did not reach the primary documents, so they are omitted from the cited list.
- No post-2020 official US equity HFT share located; widely quoted "60-75% algorithmic" figures come from vendor blogs.

## 3. How have systematic CTAs / trend followers performed over the long run?

### Takeaway
Professional trend following is profitable over decades but modest and lumpy: index-level returns in the low-to-mid single digits a year in recent decades, one standout year (2022, about +20%), and multi-year flat or losing stretches with index drawdowns in the high teens.

### Cited Findings
- SG CTA Index returned about +20.1% in 2022, its best year on record (previous best about +15.75%) — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/flat-december-sees-ctas-complete-record-year-2022/)
- SG CTA Index lost almost 3% in 2023 — [StoneX](https://www.stonex.com/en/insights/ctas-on-course-for-positive-january-returns/)
- 2025: SG CTA Index was down more than 8% year-to-date by late May 2025 before recovering in H2 — [Alternatives Watch, Jun 2025](https://www.alternativeswatch.com/2025/06/02/trend-following-hedge-funds-endure-more-pain-societe-generale-indices/); [Alternatives Watch, Sep 2025](https://www.alternativeswatch.com/2025/09/02/ctas-trend-following-hedge-funds-rebound-h1-rout-societe-generale-indices/)
- SG Trend Index ended 2025 at about +2.4%; Barclay CTA Index provisional 2025 about +3.16%; Barclay CTA 2024 +3.42%; Barclay CTA 2023 -0.39% — [The Full FX](https://thefullfx.com/ctas-end-2025-on-a-positive-note/); [The Full FX (2024)](https://thefullfx.com/currency-traders-end-year-on-strong-note/)
- Long-run Barclay CTA Index: average annual return 11.55% for Jan 1980-Nov 2010 (arithmetic; early years had only 15 programs vs 522 by Dec 2017, so early figures are less representative) — [Efficient Capital](https://www.efficient.com/managed-futures/why-managed-futures); [CME Group paper](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/files/prospects-for-ctas-in-a-rising-interest-rate-environment.pdf)
- A managed-futures broker's analysis puts typical CTA index statistics at roughly 4.5% compound annual return, 11% volatility and 18% maximum drawdown (vendor source; index not clearly identified) — [RCM Alternatives](https://www.rcmalternatives.com/?p=38199)
- SG CTA Index volatility since 2000: 8.7% — [iMGP](https://www.imgp.com/documents/iMGP_The_Case_for_Managed_Futures_in_an_Investment_Portfolio.pdf)
- Dispersion between CTA managers is large, so the index hides wide winner/loser spread — [CFM](https://www.cfm.com/steady-trends-the-reality-of-cta-return-dispersion/)

### Inferences
- Barclay CTA annual returns for 2023-2025 were about -0.4%, +3.4%, +3.2%: three years of professional systematic trading produced less than cash yields over the same period.
- The high 1980s-90s averages reflect a small, survivor-heavy sample and high interest on collateral; post-2000 results are a better guide to what the strategy earns.
- Realistic professional expectation for diversified systematic trend following: single-digit annual returns, about 10% volatility, drawdowns of 15-20% lasting years.

### Gaps
- SG CTA Index full-year 2024 and 2025 returns and its annualised return since 2000 were not found in accessible sources (Societe Generale data is not openly published).
- Barclay CTA compound return and worst drawdown over the full 1980-2025 history not confirmed from BarclayHedge directly.

## 4. Typical annual returns for quant hedge funds overall, versus a retail hope of 5-10% per month

### Takeaway
Index-level hedge fund returns are around 10% in a very good year, and systematic sub-indices were roughly flat in 2025; nothing at the index level approaches 5-10% per month.

### Cited Findings
- HFRI Fund Weighted Composite Index: about +12.6% in 2025, its strongest calendar year since 2009 — [HFR](https://www.hfr.com/media/market-commentary/hfri-surges-to-strongest-annual-gain-since-2009-as-risks-opportunities-evolve/)
- HFRI Macro: Systematic Diversified Index: about -0.65% in 2025; HFRI Macro (Total) about +7.2%; discretionary directional about +15.75% — [UBS hedge fund bulletin, Dec 2025](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/assetmanagement/insights/asset-class-perspectives/hedge-funds/articles/uga-hf-bulletin-dec-2025.html); [The Full FX](https://thefullfx.com/macro-funds-end-2025-strongly-as-hedge-funds-thrive/)
- Elite multi-strategy quant benchmark: D.E. Shaw Composite 12.9% annualised net since 2001 — [Reuters via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2600609750)
- Best ever recorded: Medallion 39.1% a year net, 63.3% compound gross (1988-2018) — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1k2fymby99nj0/Famed-Medallion-Fund-Stretches-Explanation-to-the-Limit-Professor-Claims)

### Inferences
- 5% per month compounds to about 80% a year and 10% per month to about 214% a year (arithmetic: 1.05^12 and 1.10^12). Both exceed Medallion's net return, about 6-16x D.E. Shaw's long-run rate, and are far above any hedge fund index.
- In 2025 the average systematic macro fund lost money in a year when the broad hedge fund index had its best year since 2009: being automated is not an edge by itself.

### Gaps
- No long-run (10-20 year) annualised figure retrieved for a dedicated quant/systematic equity index (e.g. HFRI or Aurum quant indices).
- Hedge fund indices suffer survivorship and backfill bias, so true averages are likely lower; no source retrieved quantifying this.

## 5. Documented automated profit in sports betting and prediction markets

### Takeaway
Yes, but concentrated in a few well-capitalised, data-heavy operations: Bill Benter's horse-racing model (close to $1bn), professional football syndicates, and on Polymarket a small set of bot-like wallets that extracted about $40m in arbitrage while most accounts lose.

### Cited Findings
- Bill Benter built a statistical model for Hong Kong horse racing and made close to $1bn; the model eventually used 120+ variables per horse and exploited the parimutuel pool; about $3m was made in the 1990-91 season (figures from Benter's own account to Bloomberg; not audited) — [Bloomberg Businessweek, May 2018](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code)
- Tony Bloom's Starlizard syndicate: a High Court claim alleges it makes about £600m a year; Bloom's lawyers dispute the figure as exaggerated; case ongoing, figure unverified — [Racing Post](https://www.racingpost.com/news/britain/high-court-case-alleges-tony-blooms-betting-empire-makes-600m-a-year-so-what-do-we-know-about-his-starlizard-syndicate-aNlkE7t8daxQ/); [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/inside-starlizard-high-court-filing-lifts-lid-on-tony-blooms-800m-betting-empire/)
- The same litigation shows syndicates need proxy ("beard") accounts to get bets on, because bookmakers restrict winning accounts — [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/epl-club-owner-tony-bloom-admits-using-proxy-betting-account-in-13m-lawsuit/)
- Polymarket: IMDEA Networks researchers analysed 86 million bets and estimated about $40m of arbitrage profit extracted (most likely Apr 2024-Apr 2025; summaries disagree on dates); top three wallets took $4.2m; most arbitrage trades earned 1-5% — [AFT 2025 paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol354-aft2025/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27.pdf); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/339958/40-million-free-money-glitch-crypto-prediction-markets); [DL News](https://dlnews.com/articles/markets/polymarket-users-lost-millions-of-dollars-to-bot-like-bettors-over-the-past-year/)
- Polymarket loser share: on-chain analyses (not peer reviewed) put unprofitable traders at 70% (DeFi Oasis, Dec 2025, 1.7m addresses) to 84.1% (2.5m wallets) — [KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/84-of-polymarket-traders-are-losing-money-0-033-capture-majority-profits)
- Institutional entry: Susquehanna became Kalshi's first official market maker with reduced fees and higher position limits; Jump Trading reportedly took small stakes in Kalshi and Polymarket in exchange for providing liquidity (Bloomberg, Feb 2026) and roughly doubled its prediction-market team to about 20; DRW also has a dedicated team — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/prop-firm-jump-trading-to-take-stakes-in-kalshi-and-polymarket-report/); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/jump-trading-prediction-markets-wall-street/); [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/electronic-market-makers-trading-on-kalshi-are-small-fish-in-a-big-pond)
- Quant firms entering prediction markets are described as hunting arbitrage rather than betting on outcomes; some prop shops remain sceptical of the opportunity size — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/wall-street-quants-move-into-prediction-markets-to-hunt-for-arbitrage-not-to-bet); [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/markets/7963620/will-kalshi-and-polymarket-win-over-prop-shops-don%E2%80%99t-bet-on-it)

### Inferences
- Prediction markets were, in 2024-25, the one venue where small automated players documentably captured arbitrage, but profits were thin per trade (1-5%), concentrated in a few wallets, and the 2026 arrival of Susquehanna, Jump and DRW with fee and limit privileges points to the window narrowing.
- Sports betting edges exist but are structurally hostile to individuals: bookmakers limit winners, so scale requires proxy accounts or exchanges/parimutuel pools with high liquidity.

### Gaps
- No audited profit figures for any betting syndicate; all are self-reported or litigation allegations.
- No reliable data on Kalshi market-maker profitability or on the share of Polymarket/Kalshi volume that is automated.
- Crypto market makers: Wintermute's CEO said the firm was profitable in 2025 without figures; reported daily volume about $15bn in 2025 falling to about $10bn in 2026; Forbes-reported profit of $582m in 2021 — [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/wintermute-plans-dollar1b-push-into-ai-and-traditional-finance-over-5-years). No audited crypto market-maker results found.

## 6. What structural advantages explain the profits, and why are they unavailable to individuals?

### Takeaway
Academic and regulatory evidence attributes HFT/market-making profits to relative speed (co-location), scale and concentration, with very high Sharpe ratios for the fastest firms; these are winner-take-most races among about half a dozen firms. Capital, data, fee tiers and research headcount explain the rest.

### Cited Findings
- Baron, Brogaard, Hagströmer & Kirilenko, "Risk and Return in High-Frequency Trading" (JFQA 2019, Swedish equities): differences in relative latency explain large differences in HFT performance; firms that improve latency rank via co-location upgrades improve trading performance — [RePEc/JFQA](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v54y2019i03p993-1024_00.html)
- Earlier CFTC-hosted drafts of the same project (E-mini S&P 500 futures): HFT revenue is concentrated in a small number of firms; one draft reports a median firm Sharpe ratio of 4.5, another an average of 9.2 — [CFTC working paper](https://www.cftc.gov/idc/groups/public/@economicanalysis/documents/file/oce_riskandreturn0414.pdf)
- Aquilina, Budish & O'Neill, "Quantifying the HFT Arms Race": latency-arbitrage races occur about once a minute per FTSE 100 symbol; top 6 firms account for over 80% of race wins and losses; stakes are about $5bn a year across global equity markets (authors' extrapolation), equal to about a 0.5 basis point tax on trading; eliminating them would cut the cost of liquidity by 17% — [BIS Working Paper 955](https://www.bis.org/publ/work955.pdf); [FCA Occasional Paper 50](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-50.pdf)
- Budish, Cramton & Shim (2015) show the speed race is a product of continuous-limit-order-book market design, producing a never-ending arms race — summarised in [NBER w29011](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29011/w29011.pdf)
- Cost structure is itself a moat: Virtu's 10-K lists brokerage, exchange, clearance fees and payments for order flow as major expenses offsetting trading income, i.e. the firm pays for retail flow and exchange access at scale — [Virtu FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1592386/000159238626000009/virt-20251231.htm)
- Designated market makers get preferential terms: Susquehanna on Kalshi receives reduced fees and higher position limits — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/electronic-market-makers-trading-on-kalshi-are-small-fish-in-a-big-pond)
- Infrastructure spend: Wintermute plans about $1bn over five years on HFT and AI data-centre infrastructure, funded from retained earnings — [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/wintermute-plans-dollar1b-push-into-ai-and-traditional-finance-over-5-years)
- Productivity of small expert teams: XTX earned about £1.71bn profit with roughly 250 staff in 2025 — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/brokers/xtx-markets-revenue-rises-43-to-393-billion-in-2025/)

### Inferences
- Speed edges are relative, not absolute: the research shows being the fastest matters, so a retail trader on a home connection or cloud server is structurally last in any race and cannot compete in latency arbitrage or passive market making on major venues.
- HFT profit per trade is tiny (fractions of a basis point); it only becomes billions through volume, rebates/fee tiers and near-zero marginal cost. An individual paying retail spreads and commissions faces the opposite cost structure.
- What remains theoretically accessible to an individual is slower, capacity-limited, low-competition niches (small prediction markets, illiquid or new venues) where institutions do not yet bother, but the 2026 institutional entry into prediction markets shows such niches get competed away.
- Professional systematic funds without a speed edge (CTAs, systematic macro) earn modest returns, which is the realistic ceiling for "slow" automated strategies even with professional research teams.

### Gaps
- No primary source retrieved quantifying exchange co-location/data fees or maker-taker rebate tiers in dollar terms.
- No ESMA report retrieved; EU-specific HFT share figures are missing.
- Research headcount and data budgets for Renaissance, Two Sigma and peers not sourced here.
